01Background

At university, grades and friendships start again and social media keeps comparison going after class. Resistance to peer influence changes little from age 18 to 30 (Steinberg & Monahan, 2007), so first-year pressure comes from the new setting rather than from age.

02Research questions

RQ1How does peer pressure shape self-comparison among first-year students?
RQ2Which weighs more on comparison: social media use or academic results?
RQ3How are peer pressure and self-comparison linked to the stress and well-being of first-year students?

03Mixed methods

Quantitative: online survey53 first-year students took a 19-item Google Form in October 2026.
Qualitative: open-ended answers22 students answered an optional open question on what would help new students feel less peer pressure.

Key open-ended answers

04Survey results n = 53

05Conclusion

RQ1

Nearly half of students (45%) often compare themselves with better-off peers and feel peer pressure. The two tend to rise together.

RQ2

Academic results weigh more: grades tie with soft skills as the top point of comparison (62%). More hours on social media did not mean more comparison.

RQ3

Negative comparison brings self-doubt (58%) and stress (49%), yet only 1 in 10 would seek help from a counsellor.
