## Bản tiếng Anh (dự phòng)

### Slide 1

Good morning, everyone. We are Group 1. Our project is about peer pressure and self-comparison in first-year university students. There are five of us, and Hân is our team leader. For the research work: Hân researched social media, wrote the proposal content and made the charts. Phúc and Minh Ngọc searched for studies, designed the survey and cleaned the data. Huyền analysed the causes, wrote our recommendations and designed the poster. Trúc summarised the theory and analysed the data. Most of our work was online. We met on Google Meet, kept our files on Google Drive, ran the survey on Google Forms and designed together on Canva. Our roles inside the team are on the next slide.

### Slide 2

Besides the research tasks, each of us also had a role in the team. Hân, our leader, managed the overall content, collected information from each member, built the timeline and gave out the tasks. Phúc supported the leader with processing information and helped with Canva design. Huyền helped find information, tracked our progress and reported back to the leader. Minh Ngọc found useful sources and finished every task we gave. Trúc is our strongest presenter and helped the whole team stay confident. In our self-assessment, every member scored 10 out of 10.

### Slide 3

So why did we choose this topic? In the first year, everything starts again: grades, friends, routines. And social media keeps the comparison going even after class. Past studies gave us three clues. First, about 31 percent of first-year students in a large study screened positive for a mental disorder, so this group needs attention. Second, 60 studies together show that friends have only a small direct effect on what we do. Third, people often think their peers are happier than they really are. So the pressure may come more from how we see our peers than from our peers. Based on this, we asked three questions. RQ1: how does peer pressure shape self-comparison? RQ2: which matters more, social media or grades? And RQ3: how are both linked to stress and well-being? Our base theory is Festinger's social comparison theory.

### Slide 4

Here is how we did it, in five steps. First, we read seven peer-reviewed studies, and we checked every number against the original paper. Second, we presented our proposal on 22 September and made our questions clearer after feedback. Third, we built a 19-question Google Form. It was anonymous and open from 6 to 8 October. Fourth, we checked the 53 answers, cleaned the data and turned it into percentages and charts. Fifth, we made the poster and revised it after our instructor's feedback on 27 October. We used mixed methods: numbers from scale and multiple-choice questions, and words from one open question that 22 students answered. Most answers came from Business students, then IT and engineering.

### Slide 5

Now our results. Nearly half of the students, 45 percent, often compare themselves with peers who seem better off. What do they compare? Grades and GPA come first, at 62 percent, tied with soft skills. After comparing, 58 percent feel self-doubt. And the result that surprised us most: only 11 percent would see a counsellor, while 30 percent keep it to themselves. For our questions: RQ1, students who feel more pressure also compare more. RQ2, grades matter more than screen time, and comparison was actually highest at three to five hours online, not for the heaviest users. RQ3, comparison brings self-doubt and stress, but very few students look for help. Please note this is one survey, so it shows patterns, not causes.

### Slide 6

Finally, what did we learn? Time management: we had a weekly plan, but the survey still ran one week late. Now we leave extra time before each deadline. Communication: we worked in pairs and met online. When our form did not fully match our questions, we talked it through and agreed on a new plan together. Learning to learn online: we learned to find and read research papers in English, build an online survey and design a poster together. About AI: we used AI to find and summarise sources, check facts against the papers, clean the data, make charts and plan the layout of our poster and slides. But we chose the topic, wrote the survey, collected every answer and decided what the results mean. Thank you for listening. We are happy to take your questions.
